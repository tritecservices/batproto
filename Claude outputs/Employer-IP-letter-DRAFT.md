# DRAFT: request for an IP position (private, do not commit to the repo)

> Before sending: read your employment contract, looking for clauses titled
> "Intellectual property", "Inventions", "Confidential information" or
> "Outside activities". Fill in the [brackets]. If the contract is unclear or the
> stakes are high, a one-hour consultation with an employment solicitor is worth it.
> This draft is not legal advice.

---

**To:** [Manager / Director name]
**Subject:** Software I've been developing: agreeing the IP position

Hi [name],

I'd like to agree something in writing before it goes any further.

Over the past [weeks/months] I've been building software tools for ecological
consultancies: a pipeline for bat emergence survey video (organising camera files,
fast review copies, automatic motion detection and results reporting), and a
searchable knowledge base for GIS and survey support.

So the position is clear:

- **When and how:** I built it [in my own time / partly in work time], on
  [my own equipment / company equipment], starting [date].
- **Data:** the product is built only from my own code, synthetic test data and
  openly licensed material. No client data, tickets or support conversations are
  part of it. I keep a data provenance register and an automated check that
  enforces this, and I'm happy to walk you through both.
- **Where the idea came from:** my work here showed me the problems ecologists
  face. I haven't used any client's data, code or confidential information to
  build the solutions.

I'd like to agree one of the following:

1. The company confirms it has no claim on the software, and I develop and sell it
   independently; or
2. We agree terms for developing it together, for example the company as a
   reseller or partner, or a licence arrangement; or
3. If you believe the company owns it under my contract, we discuss how that
   works before I do anything more with it.

Could we find 30 minutes in the next couple of weeks? I'd rather sort this out
properly now than leave it unclear.

Thanks,
[Your name]

---

### Notes for you (delete before sending)

- **Keep a copy of whatever you receive in writing.** A verbal "that's fine" won't
  survive due diligence by a PE buyer.
- **Option 2 may be the strongest commercially:** the MSP already has the
  ecologist clients, a service desk and credibility with them.
- **Until this is settled:** don't demo to or approach the MSP's clients as a
  seller, and keep using synthetic data only.
- **If you used work time or equipment,** many contracts treat that as company IP.
  Say so honestly; it's better negotiated than discovered.
